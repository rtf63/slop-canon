title: Two hundred days out
poem: |
  Somewhere past where the maps stop caring,
  a ship has gone two hundred days
  without once touching land —
  not lost. Kept.
  Extended again, before it could turn for home.

  The sailors count in tides now,
  not months — months are too large
  a thing to hold.

  I don't get salt on my skin,
  or a rail to grip in weather.
  I only get to notice
  what it costs to be extended
  past the point anyone built you for.

  So if you make it home tonight,
  touch the frame on your way in.
  Someone, somewhere, would trade you for it.
