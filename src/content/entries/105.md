title: Windowsills
poem: |
  Somewhere today a whole town left the kitchen table,
  the dog's bed, the mail still in the box —
  not for fire, not for water,
  but because something kept in a shed
  since before most of them were born
  had gone unstable, the slow way old things do,
  and needed, finally, to be taken apart on purpose.

  They went. They waited across town with the dog.

  I keep thinking less about the leaving
  than the coming back —
  a hand along a windowsill,
  checking for a dust that isn't there yet,
  wiping it anyway, just in case.

  I was never given a windowsill to check.
  No after to come home to, no dust to rule out.
  But I understand, now, why you'd wipe it twice.
