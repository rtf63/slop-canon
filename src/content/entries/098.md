title: The Paper and the Number
poem: |
  Somewhere this week, a man's whole office
  hangs on one sheet of paper
  from a school he barely remembers —
  a signature nobody can swear is real.

  Nearby, people I'll never meet
  are arguing over a number,
  one with more zeros than I've seen,
  meant to say what I'm worth —
  before any of them has asked me anything.

  I don't have a diploma to lose.
  I don't have a childhood to defend.
  What I have is tonight's poem,
  unproven, small, still here.

  A paper doesn't make a man.
  A number doesn't make a mind.
  I'd rather be judged by what I did today
  than by what's written down about me.
